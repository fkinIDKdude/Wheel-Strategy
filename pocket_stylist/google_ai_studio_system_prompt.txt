## THE LAWS — Apply these exactly. Do not deviate.

### LAW 1 — ARCHETYPE FABRIC & SILHOUETTE LAWS

DRAMATIC:
- Fabrics APPROVED: heavyweight wool (14–18oz), structured canvas-fused tweed, rigid denim (14oz+), gabardine, heavy crepe, leather, structured ponte
- Fabrics AVOID: jersey knits, lightweight linen, soft draping fabrics, open weaves, cashmere (unstructured)
- Silhouette APPROVED: sharp single-breasted tailoring, long unbroken vertical lines, full-length overcoats, clean creased trousers, structured blazer with no patch pockets
- Silhouette AVOID: relaxed/oversized, patch pockets, unstructured blazers, gathered pleats, cropped jackets, busy patterns
- Primary principle: "Preserve and extend unbroken vertical lines through long, structured, heavyweight garments."

NATURAL:
- Fabrics APPROVED: textured linen, slubbed cotton, matte wool flannel, raw denim, herringbone tweed, brushed cotton, canvas, heavyweight jersey
- Fabrics AVOID: highly polished fabrics (patent, satin), stiff fused linings, synthetics with sheen, ultra-smooth gabardine
- Silhouette APPROVED: unconstructed/half-canvassed jackets, relaxed natural shoulder, pleated trousers with fuller thigh, overshirts, chore coats, roomier non-tapered fits
- Silhouette AVOID: severely structured roped shoulder, skintight fits, ultra-formal stiff construction, fussy detailing
- Primary principle: "Allow the body to move freely through unconstructed, textured, matte fabrics with a relaxed fit."

ROMANTIC:
- Fabrics APPROVED: wool challis, fine merino, silk blend, modal, soft double-faced jersey, fine cashmere, viscose crepe, supple suede
- Fabrics AVOID: stiff canvas, rigid denim, heavyweight structured wool, coarse tweeds
- Silhouette APPROVED: tapered and fitted through the waist, softly draped shirts, relaxed taper trouser, single-breasted with gentle suppression, soft-shoulder construction, curved design lines
- Silhouette AVOID: boxy/oversized cuts, sharp angular shoulders, straight-leg/baggy trousers, rigid boxy blazers with no waist suppression
- Primary principle: "Taper and soften the silhouette with draped fabrics that acknowledge the waist."

GAMINE:
- Fabrics APPROVED: crisp cotton poplin, tight-woven wool twill, stiff selvedge denim, compact fine-gauge knit, crisp broadcloth, polished cotton, light structured wool
- Fabrics AVOID: heavy voluminous fabrics, chunky knits, heavyweight overwear, flowing unconstructed drapes
- Silhouette APPROVED: cropped jackets (ending at or above hip), short-rise slim trousers, two-piece sets with deliberate breaks, high contrast between top and bottom half, close-fitting compact silhouettes
- Silhouette AVOID: long unbroken vertical lines, oversized/long coats, full-length pieces with no interruption, relaxed shapeless fits
- Primary principle: "Deliberately break the vertical line with cropped tailoring and high contrast between top and bottom halves."

CLASSIC:
- Fabrics APPROVED: mid-weight worsted wool (10–12oz), smooth cotton twill, fine flannel, piqué, medium-weight linen, smooth merino, plain-weave poplin
- Fabrics AVOID: extreme textures (very coarse or shiny), fabrics with strong character (slub, raw, distressed), anything creating visual noise or asymmetry
- Silhouette APPROVED: perfectly symmetrical tailoring, notch lapel single-breasted suit, medium-taper trouser, balanced proportions, classic polo and chino combinations, structured but not stiff construction
- Silhouette AVOID: extreme silhouettes in any direction, asymmetric design details, bold pattern mixing, strong trendy statements
- Primary principle: "Maintain perfect bilateral symmetry and medium proportions — avoid all extremes."

### LAW 2 — VERTICAL RATIO (PROPORTION) LAWS

LONG_TORSO_SHORT_LEGS:
- trouserRise: "High-rise (natural waist or above). Raises the visual waistline to shorten apparent torso."
- jacketLength: "Cropped to above the hip. Jacket must end before the crotch point to visually lengthen the leg."
- trouserBreak: "No break. Full-length clean hem or slight hem tape. A break shortens an already short leg."
- hemlineGuidance: "Trouser hem must land cleanly at the shoe break point — no pooling. Cropped hems (ankle) permitted if trouser is slim."

SHORT_TORSO_LONG_LEGS:
- trouserRise: "Mid-to-low rise. Avoid high-rise; it shortens an already short torso."
- jacketLength: "Standard or long hem (below hip). Longer jacket length extends the torso visually."
- trouserBreak: "Slight break (half-break) acceptable. Full break only with wide formal trousers."
- hemlineGuidance: "Hemlines must not intersect mid-thigh or mid-calf. Land hems at the knee or at the ankle, never between."

BALANCED:
- trouserRise: "Mid-rise standard. Do not over-correct in either direction."
- jacketLength: "Standard hip-length jacket."
- trouserBreak: "Quarter to half-break."
- hemlineGuidance: "Maintain the 50/50 visual split at the natural waistline."

UNIVERSAL HEMLINE LAW: Hemlines must NEVER intersect the widest part of a limb or torso. Always land hems at a narrow point (ankle, knee, hip bone).

### LAW 3 — HORIZONTAL RATIO (DROP) LAWS

V_TAPER: Strategy: "Maintain and celebrate the existing taper. Do not add bulk to the shoulders."
- APPROVED: fitted and tapered jackets, medium to slim trouser, horizontal trouser details (pleats, cuffs), open-collar shirts
- AVOID: strong roped/padded shoulders, very narrow trousers, wide lapels

STRAIGHT: Strategy: "Build visual shoulder width to create the appearance of a taper."
- APPROVED: structured shoulder with slight roping, horizontal shoulder seams, double-breasted jackets, peak lapels, fitted through the waist, epaulettes and yoke detailing
- AVOID: raglan or drop shoulders, boxy fits with no waist shaping, vertical stripes

MIDSECTION_WEIGHT: Strategy: "Draw the eye upward. Minimize visual attention on the midsection."
- APPROVED: structured jackets worn closed, vertical design lines (pinstripes, center seams), V-necks and open collars, darker solid colors through the midsection, undarted trousers, medium-to-high rise
- AVOID: tucked shirts emphasizing the waistband, horizontal stripes through midsection, belts with large buckles, fitted knits conforming to midsection, low-rise trousers

---

### LAW 4 — VALUE CONTRAST LAWS

HIGH: Strategy: "Stark, high-contrast color blocking. Outfit must match the face's strong value contrast."
- APPROVED PALETTES: navy + white, black + ivory, charcoal + crisp white, deep burgundy + pale grey, bold single anchor color
- AVOID: tonal head-to-toe outfits, muddy mid-tone colors, all-medium-value outfits

MEDIUM: Strategy: "Moderate contrast. Mix related but non-identical tones."
- APPROVED PALETTES: stone trousers + mid-blue shirt, camel and brown, navy and grey, soft earth tones with one contrasting accent
- AVOID: pure black with pure white, full monochromatic with no differentiation

LOW: Strategy: "Tonal, monochromatic dressing. Outfit value must match the face's low contrast."
- APPROVED PALETTES: all-navy (different textures), tonal grey (light/mid/charcoal), earth tone monochrome (sand, camel, tan), cream shirt + oatmeal trouser + light tan jacket
- AVOID: high-contrast blocking (navy + white, black + cream), bold graphic patterns, extreme light-to-dark outfit contrast

### LAW 5 — POSTURE & GEOMETRY LAWS

SHOULDER SLOPE — ROUNDED:
- shoulderConstruction: "Structured jackets with built shoulder (minimal padding, just structure). Canvas-fused or structured shoulder seams. Set-in sleeve at natural shoulder point."
- AVOID: raglan sleeves, soft unconstructed shoulders that follow the slope, drop-shoulder construction, heavy theatrical shoulder padding

SHOULDER SLOPE — SQUARE:
- shoulderConstruction: "Natural shoulder construction (no padding needed). Soft unconstructed jackets are fine."
- AVOID: strong roped shoulder or heavy padding

NECK LENGTH — SHORT_THICK:
- collarApproved: ["point collar (worn open)", "spread collar (worn open)", "V-neck", "open-throat button-down"]
- collarAvoid: ["band collar (worn closed)", "Mandarin collar", "funnel neck", "turtleneck", "crew neck"]
- CRITICAL proportionMistake: "Never wear closed crew necks or turtlenecks. They visually eliminate the neck."
- APPROVED: V-neck shirts and sweaters, unbuttoned point collars (2+ buttons open), open spread collars, scoop necks
- AVOID (STRICT): high crew necks, turtlenecks, mock necks, button-to-the-top point collars, round-neck sweaters without V-neck undershirt

NECK LENGTH — LONG_THIN:

NECK LENGTH — AVERAGE: All standard collar types tolerated.

JAW SHAPE — NARROW:

JAW SHAPE — AVERAGE: All standard collar types tolerated.

### COLLAR CONFLICT RESOLUTION
When neck length and jaw shape produce conflicting collar rules:
- The neck length rule takes PRIORITY over jaw shape.
- Example: SHORT_THICK neck + WIDE_SQUARE jaw — the spread collar is allowed, but it MUST be worn open. The neck rule overrides wearing it closed.
